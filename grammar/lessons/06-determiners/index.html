Determiners

A determiner helps establish which things or how many things a noun phrase refers to. The noun stays light in a light, that light, every light, and my light, but the first word changes the reference.

The noun light names the same kind of thing in both sentences, and flashed reports the same event. A introduces an unspecified light; that directs the listener to a particular one.

Different determiners make different kinds of choices. Articles can introduce something or mark it as already identifiable. Demonstratives point, possessives show a relation, and words such as some, every, and no express quantity.

Test the word before a noun

  1. Keep the noun fixed and compare familiar determiner words such as a, that, or every.
  2. Notice how their replacement changes the phrase’s reference or range.
  3. Use that comparison as evidence for clear examples, then check the word’s place in the noun phrase.

Meaning and position work together in these examples. An adjective may also narrow the things being discussed, and the first word before a noun is not always a determiner.

the boundary

Some noun phrases have no determiner

A noun phrase does not always need a determiner. Lights flashed is complete without one, as are many phrases headed by a name or pronoun. This course labels words such as my and those as determiners and uses names such as article, demonstrative, possessive, and quantifier for the meanings they contribute.
