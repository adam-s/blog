Form is not function

Form names the kind of word or phrase. Function names the job that unit does in a larger phrase or clause. One form can do several jobs, and one job can be filled by several forms.

Both highlighted units are PPs because each has a preposition and a complement. Their parents differ: one belongs under another preposition, and one belongs with the clause’s predicate. The preposition, the meaning of place, and final position do not by themselves choose that relationship.

a narrower consequence

Requiredness belongs to the verb frame

Both final phrases are PPs and both are adverbials in the course model. With rehearsed, the event remains complete without the location. In the ordinary caused-placement sense of put, the destination is part of the verb’s required frame. Removal tests that frame in this meaning and context; it is not a test for PP form or for every function.

Ask two separate questions.

First ask what kind of unit the words form. Then ask what that complete unit does in the structure that contains it. The answers describe different relationships, so a phrase needs both.
